Research theme

Environment, CGM, Galaxy Quenching

This theme explores how the large-scale environment modulates the angular momentum of circumgalactic gas, shaping whether gas can flow efficiently into galaxies or help keep them quenched.

Featured Papers

Featured paper

From large-scale environment to CGM angular momentum to star-forming activities - I. Star-forming galaxies

Wang et al. show that star-forming galaxies in IllustrisTNG can experience repeated star-forming and quiescent episodes. These cycles are tied to a synchronized breathing motion of the CGM: cold gas can spiral inward to fuel star formation, while feedback and environmental interactions later push the system back toward a quieter phase.

Featured paper

From large-scale environment to CGM angular momentum to star-forming activities - II. Quenched galaxies

Lu et al. extend the picture to present-day quenched galaxies. The paper finds that quenched systems have long lived in environments and CGM reservoirs with higher angular momentum, making gas inflow less efficient. This provides a route by which galaxies can remain quenched after the initial shutdown.
